Turan University is a core component of a corporation that represents a
voluntary association of higher, secondary professional, and secondary educational
institutions, research centers, and public organizations.
The main goal of the Turan Educational Corporation is to create a unique
integrated environment for the accumulation of knowledge, competencies, and
professional skills aimed at training highly demanded specialists in various fields.
This is achieved through the use of creative potential as well as material, technical,
financial, and organizational resources.
The Turan Educational Corporation is a unified system that ensures the
principles of continuity and multi-level education. It includes a school-lyceum, two
colleges, two universities, and the educational and health complex Tau-Turan
located in the cities of Almaty and Astana.
This approach to youth education allows for more efficient use of the
corporation’s scientific, educational, and material-technical potential, creating the
necessary conditions for lifelong learning, professional development, and student
involvement in research and entrepreneurial activities.
The corporation is headed by Dr. Rakhman Alshanov, Doctor of Economic
Sciences, Professor, Honored Worker of the Republic of Kazakhstan, recipient of
the “Parasat” and “Barys” (III degree) orders, President of the Association of
Universities of the Republic of Kazakhstan, President of the Academy of Economic
Sciences of the Republic of Kazakhstan, and member of the Board of the Ministry
of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan.

Preface
This Academic Policy defines the procedures for organizing the educational
process based on the credit-based learning system for bachelor’s, master’s, and
doctoral programs at Turan University. The document includes general provisions,
principles of the university’s functioning as an innovative and entrepreneurial
institution, procedures for student registration and state scholarship payments;
requirements for mastering educational programs; regulations on current, midterm,
and final assessments; the organization of student internships; evaluation of
students’ academic achievements; and the rules governing student transfers,
reinstatement, participation in academic mobility programs, academic leave,
expulsion, and final attestation, among others.
The Academic Policy of Turan University has been developed in accordance
with the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Education” (No. 319-III dated July
27, 2007); the Model Rules for the Activities of Higher and (or) Postgraduate
Education Organizations (approved by Order No. 595 of the Ministry of Education
and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan, dated October 31, 2018); the Rules for
the Organization of the Educational Process under the Credit Technology of
Education (Order No. 152, dated April 20, 2011); the Rules for the Organization and
Implementation of Educational-Methodological and Scientific-Methodological
Work (Order No. 583, dated November 29, 2007); and the Model Rules for the
Organization of the Work of Collegial Management Bodies in Higher and (or)
Postgraduate Education Organizations (Order No. 157 of the Ministry of Science
and Higher Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan, dated April 1, 2025), as well
as other normative and instructional documents and orders of the MSHE RK (in their
current editions), the Model of the President of the Republic of Kazakhstan’s “Altyn
Sapa” Award, and internal regulatory documents of Turan University.
The Academic Policy of Turan University is also developed in alignment with
international documents in the field of quality education, including: the “European
Higher Education Area”; the Joint Statement of European Ministers of Education
(Bologna, June 19, 1999); “The Bologna Process 2020: The European Higher
Education Area in the New Decade”; the Communiqué of the Conference of
European Ministers Responsible for Higher Education (Leuven / Louvain-la-Neuve,
April 28–29, 2009); the Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance in the
European Higher Education Area (ESG, 2015); the EFQM Excellence Model
(2013); ISO 9001:2015; and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
1. Glossary
1. Academic mobility refers to the movement of students or teaching-research staff for the
purpose of studying or conducting research for a specific academic period (a semester or
an academic year) at another higher and/or postgraduate education institution (either
within the country or abroad), with the mandatory transfer of completed educational
programs or courses in the form of academic credits to their home higher and/or
postgraduate education institution, or for the continuation of studies at another higher
and/or postgraduate education institution;
2. Academic leave is a period during which learners in educational institutions (students,
cadets, master’s students, adjuncts, cadets, trainees, doctoral students, interns, or
residents) temporarily suspend their studies for medical reasons.
3. Academic freedom is the set of rights granted to participants in the educational process,
allowing them to independently determine the content of education for elective courses,
additional types of training, and the organization of educational activities. Its purpose is
to create conditions for the creative development of students and teachers, as well as for
the application of innovative technologies and teaching methods
4. Academic integrity is a set of values and principles that reflect a student’s honesty in the
learning process–when completing written assignments (such as tests, essays, theses, or
dissertations), answering during exams, conducting research, expressing personal views,
interacting with academic staff, instructors, and fellow students, as well as in the process
of assessment and evaluation.
5. Academic backlog refers to the presence, in a student’s academic record, of courses that
have not been studied or courses with an unsatisfactory final assessment grade, in
accordance with the curriculum.
6. Academic degree is a qualification awarded by educational institutions to students who
have successfully completed the corresponding educational programs and passed the final
attestation.
7. Academic difference is the discrepancy between the courses listed in the working
curricula, determined by the host educational institution based on the list of completed
courses, their syllabi, and their volume in academic hours or credits as reflected in the
transcript.
8. Academic calendar is a schedule that outlines the timing of educational and assessment
activities, as well as professional internships, throughout the academic year, indicating
days of rest (holidays and vacations).
9. Academic Committee is a collegial consultative and advisory body responsible for
ensuring academic teaching standards and the professional development of faculty
members. It promotes the implementation of advanced external higher education standards
and provides consultations and recommendations on strategic initiatives related to
teaching and the overall quality of university education.
10. Academic credit is a standardized unit of measurement that reflects the volume of
academic and/or research work (workload) completed by a student and/or instructor.
11. Academic period (Term) is a period of theoretical instruction determined independently
by the educational institution in one of three forms: semester, trimester, or quarter.
12. Academic rating of a student (Rating) is a quantitative indicator of the level of a student’s
mastery of the course curriculum, determined based on the results of interim (midterm)
assessment.
13. Academic hour is a unit of measurement for the volume of classes or other types of
academic work. One academic hour equals 50 minutes and is used in the preparation of
the academic calendar (study schedule), class timetables, planning and recording of
completed coursework, as well as in determining teaching loads and accounting for
instructors’ work.
14. Active handouts are visual and illustrative materials distributed during classes to motivate
students toward creative and effective mastery of the topic. They may include lecture
summaries, references, slides, examples, glossaries, and assignments for independent
work.
15. Appeal is a procedure conducted to identify and correct cases of unfair or biased
assessment of a student’s academic performance.
16. Student internship bases are organizations whose statutory activities correspond to the
profile of specialist training and the requirements of the educational program. Such
organizations possess qualified personnel to supervise professional practice and have the
necessary material and technical resources.
17. Undergraduate study (Bachelor’s degree program) is a level of higher education aimed at
training specialists with the award of the degree of Bachelor in a corresponding
educational program, requiring the completion of at least 240 academic credits.
18. Bachelor is an academic degree awarded to individuals who have completed an
undergraduate (higher education) program.
19. Point-rating letter grading system is a system for assessing the level of academic
achievement in points, corresponding to the internationally accepted letter grading scale
with a numerical equivalent, which allows for determining students’ academic ratings.
20. External assessment of academic achievements (EAAA) is a form of independent
monitoring of the quality of education.
21. Graduate school is the main academic and research structural unit of the university that
provides training and graduation of specialists in one or several related fields of study in
accordance with the classifier of higher and postgraduate education specialties. It carries
out educational, methodological, research, instructional, and social activities, as well as
the training and professional development of academic and teaching staff.
22. Dual-degree education is the opportunity for simultaneous study under two educational
programs and curricula, with the aim of obtaining two equivalent diplomas or one main
and one additional diploma.
23. Department of Academic Affairs is the academic service of Turan University responsible
for planning, managing, and monitoring the educational process. It ensures the recording
of the complete history of a student’s academic achievements, organizes all forms of
knowledge assessment, and calculates the student’s academic rating.
24. Descriptors are descriptions of the level and scope of knowledge, skills, abilities, and
competencies acquired by students upon completion of an educational program at a
corresponding level (stage) of higher or postgraduate education. Descriptors are based on
learning outcomes, developed competencies, and the total number of credits (academic
units) earned.
25. Diploma thesis/project is a final graduation work that summarizes the results of a student’s
independent study and research on a relevant issue within a specific specialty of the
corresponding field of science.
26. Distance learning technology (DLT) is a technology based on the use of technical devices
and remote access tools that enable the educational process to be conducted indirectly. It
is applied within the full-time (on-campus) form of study.
27. Doctoral studies (PhD program) are a professional postgraduate educational program
aimed at training scientific and pedagogical personnel, with a standard duration of study
of at least three years and the awarding of an academic degree.
28. Doctor of Philosophy (PhD), Doctor of Profile (Doctor) are degrees awarded to
individuals who have completed professional doctoral study programs in the
corresponding specialties.
29. Doctoral dissertation is a scholarly work representing an independent research study that
develops theoretical propositions qualifying as a new scientific achievement or solves a
scientific problem of significant socio-cultural or economic importance. It may also
present scientifically grounded technical, economic, or technological solutions, the
implementation of which makes a substantial contribution to the development of the
country’s economy.
30. Additional types of training are components of the educational program that include
various forms of professional internships, physical education, military training, and other
related activities.
31. European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS) is a method of assigning
credit units (credits) to components of educational programs (subjects, courses, modules),
which enables the comparison and transfer of completed academic disciplines (with
corresponding credits and grades) when a student changes their educational trajectory,
institution, or country of study.
32. Enrollment is the procedure of pre-registering students for academic courses (subjects).
33. Overseas scientific internship is the participation of master’s and doctoral students in
educational or scientific institutions abroad for the purpose of studying and/or collecting
materials to carry out research or experimental studies related to their dissertation topic.
34. Defense of the final project is the public presentation by a student of their completed
diploma project/thesis, master’s dissertation/project, or doctoral dissertation at a meeting
of the attestation commission or dissertation council, following which a decision is made
on the awarding of an academic or scientific degree.
35. Individual educational trajectory is the independent selection, planning, and mastery by a
student of the educational content in accordance with their individual study plan within
the framework of a higher or postgraduate education program.
36. Individual study plan is a study plan developed by a student for each academic year
independently, with the assistance of an academic advisor, based on the educational
program and its accompanying course catalog.
37. Information technology for distance learning is a technology used for the creation,
transmission, and storage of educational materials on electronic media, as well as for
organizing and supporting the learning process through information and
telecommunication technologies.
38. Information and educational portal is a systematically organized, interconnected set of
Internet information resources and services containing administrative, academic, and
educational-methodological information that enables the organization of the educational
process for distance learning.
39. Information and educational resources are formalized educational ideas and knowledge,
various data, as well as methods and means of their accumulation, storage, and exchange
between sources and consumers of information.
40. Artificial Intelligence (AI) – an information and communication technology that enables
the imitation or surpassing of human cognitive functions for the purpose of performing
intellectual tasks and finding solutions.
41. Research Internship – a type of professional practice for master’s and doctoral students
conducted to familiarize them with the latest theoretical, methodological, and
technological achievements of domestic and international science, modern research
methods, data processing and interpretation, as well as to strengthen practical skills in
applying these methods in their dissertation research.
42. Final attestation of students (Qualification Examination) is a procedure for assessing
graduates’ theoretical knowledge, acquired professional competencies, readiness to
perform professional tasks, and the extent to which their training meets the requirements
of the educational program.
43. Final assessment is the evaluation of students’ academic achievements aimed at assessing
the quality of their mastery of a course’s curriculum. It is conducted during the period of
interim attestation in the form of an exam. If the course spans multiple academic periods,
the final assessment may cover only the portion of the course studied in the given period.
44. ESG (Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance) – international standards and
guidelines for ensuring the quality of higher and postgraduate education within the
European Higher Education Area.
45. Integral Grade Point Average (IGPA) – a comprehensive assessment of a student’s
academic achievements.
46. Student’s research achievements (iROS – indicators of Research-Oriented Study) – the
collection of research skills and competencies acquired by a student through research and
project activities (including participation in hackathons and startups), measured by
specific indicators and reflecting the student’s average score in research skills.
47. Catalog of elective modules (courses) – a systematically organized annotated list of all
modules (courses) in the elective component, containing a brief description of each,
including the learning objectives, a summary of the content (main sections), and expected
learning outcomes (knowledge, skills, abilities, and competencies to be acquired by
students). The catalog of elective modules (courses) is part of the Course Catalog of the
educational program.
48. Course Catalog – a systematically organized annotated list of all courses in the elective
component, containing a brief description of each course, including its learning objectives,
a summary of the content (main sections), and expected learning outcomes (knowledge,
skills, abilities, and competencies to be acquired by students). The catalog also specifies
the prerequisites and corequisites for each course. It provides students with the opportunity
to make alternative choices among elective courses
49. Quality Committee – a collegial supervisory body that monitors management processes
and ensures the quality of academic activities within the internal quality assurance system
based on ESG standards.
50. Elective component – a list of courses and their corresponding minimum credit (or
academic hour) requirements offered by higher education institutions, which students
independently choose in any academic period, taking into account course prerequisites
and corequisites.
51. Competencies – the ability of students to practically apply the knowledge, skills, and
abilities acquired during their studies in professional activities.
52. Assessment of students’ academic achievements – the evaluation of students’ knowledge
through various forms of control (current, midterm, and final) and attestation, determined
independently by the higher education institution.
53. Credit (Credit-hour) – a standardized unit of measurement for the volume of academic
work completed by a student or instructor.
54. Credit-based learning technology – an educational approach based on students’ choice and
independent planning of the sequence of courses, using credits as a standardized unit to
measure the academic workload of both students and instructors.
55. Summer semester – an academic period outside the regular academic year, organized to
meet students’ needs for additional learning, the elimination of academic backlog or
differences in curricula, study of courses in agreement with other universities, and similar
purposes.
56. Master’s degree program – a professional postgraduate educational program aimed at
training scientific, scientific-pedagogical, and managerial personnel, leading to the award
of the academic degree of Master in the corresponding specialty, depending on the type
of training (scientific-pedagogical or professional).
57. Master is an academic degree awarded to individuals who have completed a professional
master’s degree program
58. Master’s thesis/project is a final graduation work that summarizes the results of a master’s
student’s independent research on a relevant issue within a specific specialty of the
corresponding field of science.
59. Module – an autonomous, self-contained structural element of an educational program,
defined in terms of learning outcomes, with clearly formulated knowledge, skills, abilities,
and competencies to be acquired by students, along with appropriate assessment criteria.
60. Modular educational program – a study program comprising a set of academic modules
designed to help students acquire the key competencies required for obtaining a specific
degree and/or qualification.
61. Modular learning – a method of organizing the educational process based on the modular
structure of the educational program, study plan, and courses.
62. Modular structure of an educational program – a means of achieving the objectives of
professional training by defining the content and structure of the program based on the
concept of organizing the educational process, where the learning goal is the set of
professional competencies acquired by the student.
63. Multimedia – a set of hardware and software tools that allow a user to interactively work
with various types of data, including graphics, text, audio, and video.
64. Scientific research work of a student/master’s/doctoral student (SRW/SRW-M/SRW-D)
– a mandatory and integral part of training qualified specialists at the university, forming
an inseparable component of the unified educational and scientific-innovative process
65. Scientific supervisor/consultant – an academic mentor who guides a student in the
completion of their final project (diploma work/project, master’s thesis/project, or
doctoral dissertation).
66. Educational program – an approved set of modules or course units required for awarding
a specific degree or diploma. When designing educational programs, the expected learning
outcomes are typically considered, along with a structured set of learning procedures and
situations intended to achieve these outcomes.
67. Students (Learners) – individuals enrolled in bachelor’s, master’s, or doctoral (PhD)
programs; including undergraduate students, master’s students, and doctoral candidates.
68. Core component (Core Subjects) – a list of courses and their corresponding minimum
credit requirements established by the State Educational Standards, which students are
required to complete..
69. Course description – a brief overview of a course (consisting of 5–8 sentences), including
its objectives, tasks, and content.
70. Registrar’s Office – an academic service responsible for recording students’ academic
achievements, taking into account the credits they have earned, organizing interim and
final assessments, and calculating their academic ratings.
71. Postrequisites – courses for which the knowledge, skills, and abilities acquired upon
completion of a given course are required
72. Teaching practice – a type of professional internship aimed at consolidating the theoretical
knowledge acquired by students during their higher education, developing pedagogical
skills, and mastering advanced professional and organizational practices relevant to their
future teaching specialty within an educational institution.
73. GPA for transfer – the annually established minimum level of the weighted average of
academic achievements required for a student to advance to the next year of study.
74. Credit transfer – the procedure for recognizing the equivalence of a course studied at
another educational institution or under a different curriculum with a course in the current
approved curriculum for the student’s specialty, with the course and the grade earned
being recorded in the student’s transcript.
75. Retake – the repeated completion of a course in the event of receiving a final grade of
“unsatisfactory” (“F”).
76. Pre-graduation internship – a type of professional practice conducted before the start of
the diploma project, aimed at allowing students to collect the necessary materials for
completing their bachelor’s final work.
77. Prerequisites – courses that provide the knowledge, skills, and abilities required to
successfully study a given course.
78. Course syllabus – an academic program that includes a description of the course, its
objectives and goals, a summary of the content, topics and their duration, assignments for
independent work, consultation hours, schedule of knowledge assessments, instructor
requirements, assessment criteria, and a list of references.
79. Industrial (Professional) practice – a type of professional internship aimed at consolidating
theoretical knowledge from core and specialized courses through practical, real-world
performance of a specialist’s duties. It allows students to acquire practical skills and
master advanced professional and organizational practices relevant to their future
specialty. During industrial practice, students are directly prepared for professional
activities across all areas in real production or work conditions.
80. Interim assessment of students – a procedure conducted during the examination period to
evaluate the quality of students’ mastery (knowledge, skills, abilities, and competencies)
of part or all of a course’s content after its completion.
81. Professional competence – the readiness and ability to act effectively in accordance with
the requirements of the professional field, to methodically and independently solve tasks
and problems, and to evaluate the results of one’s own activities.
82. Professional practice – a mandatory component of the educational program aimed at
consolidating theoretical knowledge, acquiring practical skills and competencies, and
mastering innovative technologies.
83. Curriculum (Work Study Plan) – a document independently developed by the university
based on the educational program and students’ individual study plans
84. Course registration – the procedure by which students enroll in courses for the upcoming
academic period or year.
85. Midterm assessment – the evaluation of students’ academic achievements at the
completion of a section (module) of a course.
86. Independent student work – tasks assigned from a specific list of topics for self-study,
supported by educational and methodological literature and recommendations, and
assessed through tests, control works, colloquia, essays, reports, or papers. Depending on
the student category, it is classified as undergraduate independent work, master’s
independent work, or doctoral independent work. The entire volume of independent work
is confirmed by assignments that require the student’s daily self-directed effort.
87. Independent student work under instructor supervision – out-of-class student work
conducted under the guidance of an instructor according to an approved schedule.
Depending on the student category, it is classified as undergraduate independent work
under instructor supervision, master’s independent work under instructor supervision, or
doctoral independent work under instructor supervision.
88. Syllabus – the academic program of a course, including a description of the course, its
objectives and goals, a summary of the content, topics and their duration, assignments for
independent work, consultation hours, schedule of student assessments, instructor
requirements, assessment criteria, and a list of references.
89. Grade Point Average (GPA) – the weighted average of a student’s academic achievements
over a specific period in a chosen program. It is calculated as the sum of the products of
credits and the numeric equivalents of final grades for all types of academic work, divided
by the total number of credits for those types of work during that period.
90. Student’s social and sports achievements (SSCI – Social and Sporty Competition
Indicators) – the set of skills and competencies acquired by a student through participation
in community service (including volunteer work), social, and sports activities, measured
by specific indicators and reflecting the student’s average score in social competencies.
91. Current assessment of students’ performance – the systematic evaluation of students’
knowledge, skills, and abilities on specific topics, sections, or modules in accordance with
the course curriculum, conducted by the instructor during in-class and out-of-class
sessions according to the schedule and/or timeline throughout the academic period.
92. Theoretical training – a component of the educational program that includes the study of
general education, core, and specialized courses according to standard curricula, as well
as state-mandated, socio-communicative, and professional modules according to the main
study plan.
93. Standard curriculum – a document that regulates the list and volume of courses in an
educational program, the sequence in which they are studied, and the forms of assessment
94. Standard course program (hereinafter – SCP) – an academic document for a course within
the core component of an educational program, which defines its content, volume, and
recommended literature in accordance with subparagraph 5-2) of Article 5 of the Law
95. Transcript – a document listing the courses completed during a specific period of study,
indicating the credits earned and the grades received in both letter and numeric form.
96. Tutor – an instructor who acts as an academic advisor to a student for mastering a specific
course.
97. Academic practice – a type of professional practice conducted during the early years of
study, combining theoretical and practical components of the educational process at the
beginning of a student’s professional development within certain foundational courses. Its
main goal is to acquire basic professional skills and abilities through independent practical
activities. It is carried out at practice bases and has an introductory, exploratory, and
research-oriented character.
98. Students’ academic achievements – the knowledge, skills, abilities, and competencies
acquired by students during their studies, reflecting their attained level of personal and
professional development.
99. Academic module – a structural element of an educational program designed for students
to achieve specific learning outcomes, which in whole or in part contribute to the
competencies they acquire.
100. Academic and methodological complex of a course – a set of regulatory and instructional
documents that define the course content, teaching and assessment tools necessary and
sufficient for the effective organization of the course within the educational program, in
accordance with the curriculum.
101. Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) – officially outlined in the document titled
“Transforming Our World: the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development”, which
includes 17 global goals, 169 associated targets, and 242 indicators.
102. Advisor – an instructor who serves as an academic mentor for a student in a specific
specialty, assisting in selecting the learning trajectory (developing an individual study
plan) and guiding the student through the educational program during their studies.
103. Examination session – the period of interim assessment for students at higher education
institutions (HEIs).
104. Elective courses – courses included in the elective component within the established credit
limits, offered by educational institutions to reflect a student’s individual preparation,
taking into account the socio-economic development and needs of a specific region, as
well as the established academic schools of the higher education institution.
105. Electronic attendance and current performance journal – an online journal in the LMS
Canvas system, continuously updated by instructors throughout the academic period.
Electronic academic achievements journal – an online journal in the Turan University
Automated Control System (АСУ «Туран»)

3. General provisions
Education at Turan University is conducted in a full-time format. The
procedure for organizing the academic process through distance learning is as
follows: the university allows a transfer to distance learning for no more than 20
percent of courses in the field of law, and for other fields of study, the transfer to
distance learning is allowed for no more than 50 percent of the total number of
courses throughout the entire period of study.
Three-level educational programs (EP) are offered: Bachelor’s, Master’s, and
Doctoral programs.
Turan University ensures equal access to education for all students, regardless
of gender, nationality, race, religion, or social status. Special conditions are
provided for persons with disabilities.
3.1 The content of educational programs is determined by the relevant statemandated educational standards and is implemented through curricula and course
programs. The mandatory component of an educational program is defined by the
standard curriculum and standard course programs. The cycles of basic (hereinafter
– BD) and major (hereinafter – MD) disciplines include university component
courses (hereinafter – UC) and elective component courses (hereinafter – EC). These
components are developed by the graduate schools based on employer requirements,
the educational needs of students, and considering trends in the development of the
respective fields of knowledge.The academic workload of students is measured in
credits, earned throughout the academic year through theoretical instruction, final
assessments, and additional forms of learning. To obtain the corresponding
academic degree, a student must complete the required number of credits. The
duration of study for bachelor’s programs is 4 years (accelerated programs – 3 or 2
years); for master’s programs: 2 years for scientific and pedagogical tracks; 1 or 1.5
years for specialized tracks. For doctoral (PhD) programs, the duration of study is 3
years.
The academic process consists of two components:
a) academic classes and independent work of students;
b) transfer of practical knowledge and skills, entrepreneurial experience, and
experience in innovative activities.
The curriculum of bachelor’s programs consists of four module blocks
1) General modules include courses from the cycles of general education
disciplines (hereinafter – GED) and basic disciplines (hereinafter – BD), which
develop general educational competencies not directly related to the specialty, as
well as social-ethical, cultural, organizational-management, and entrepreneurial
competencies. To develop entrepreneurial skills, each academic year offers
entrepreneurial modules with a workload of at least 5 credits per year. For master’s
and doctoral programs, general modules include courses from the BD cycle
2) Specialty modules include courses from the BD and major disciplines
(hereinafter – MD), forming the foundation of the specialty and aimed at developing
general professional and specialized competencies within a specific educational
program, as well as general competencies. For bachelor’s programs, these also
include professional practice (educational, industrial, and pre-graduation). The
academic year concludes with professional practice. The minimum workload for
practice throughout the entire period of study is 8 credits.For master’s programs:
pedagogical and research practice for the scientific and pedagogical track (industrial
practice for the specialized track), and research work for the scientific and
pedagogical track (experimental research work for the specialized track), including
internships. For doctoral programs: pedagogical and research practice, and doctoral
research work, including internships.
3) For Master's degree programs, students complete pedagogical and
research internships in the scientific and pedagogical track (or professional
internship in the specialized track), as well as research work in the scientific and
pedagogical track (or experimental research work in the specialized track), including
practical training (internship). For Doctoral programs, students complete
pedagogical and research internships, and conduct doctoral research work, including
practical training (internship).
4) Additional modules (Minors) that go beyond the main qualification
include courses outside the major field of study, aimed at developing additional
competencies (such as information technology, foreign languages, and others).
5) Final Assessment.
3.2 The academic year consists of academic periods, interim assessment
periods, vacations, and internships. For final-year students, the academic year also
includes the period of final assessment.
An academic period lasts 15 weeks under the semester system and 10 weeks
under the trimester system. The interim assessment period lasts at least 1 week.
Vacations are granted to students after each academic period, and the total duration
of vacation time within the academic year shall be no less than 7 weeks.
The summer semester (except for the final year) lasts at least 6 weeks and is
intended to meet the needs of additional studies, to make up academic deficiencies
or curriculum differences, to study academic disciplines and earn credits at other
universities with mandatory credit transfer to the home university, and to improve
the student’s Grade Point Average (GPA).
3.3 The duration and schedule of each academic year, as well as the
distribution of academic workload by type of activity, are specified in the academic
calendar, which is published and made available to students and faculty members no
later than three months prior to the beginning of the academic year.
For each educational program, the respective Graduate School develops a
Course Catalogue, which includes a Catalogue of Elective Disciplines (CED).
The CED provides students with the opportunity to choose elective courses
on an alternative basis in order to design an individual learning trajectory.
3.4 During periods of restrictive measures related to the spread of the
coronavirus infection, the educational process shall be organized in a blended
learning format (lectures and seminars conducted online, and laboratory or practiceoriented classes held in person, in compliance with sanitary regulations and a social
distance of 2 meters) and in a distance learning format in accordance with the special
sanitary and epidemiological regime. The Regulations on Ensuring the Sanitary and
Epidemiological Regime (SER) are developed based on the recommendations and
instructions of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of
Kazakhstan (MSHE RK), following the decisions of the Interdepartmental
Commission on Preventing the Emergence and Spread of Coronavirus Infection in
the Republic of Kazakhstan, and are approved by an order of the Rector.
Blended learning is carried out only upon receiving a positive conclusion from
the Chief State Sanitary Doctor of the city of Almaty confirming readiness for inperson classes, and upon issuance of the corresponding Rector’s order. All
participants of the blended learning process (students, faculty members, and other
university staff) are required to comply with the Regulations on Ensuring the SER
and to confirm this obligation with a personal signature upon their first visit to the
university in the current academic year.
3.5 The University shall timely inform students, faculty members, and other
university staff about the specific features of the organization of the educational
process and dormitory accommodation through publication on the University’s
official website, corporate email notifications, and online meetings between the
University administration and students, faculty, and staff.
4. Innovative Entrepreneurial University
The main principle underlying the organization of the Innovative and
Entrepreneurial University “Turan” is the harmonious integration of three
fundamental types of activity: scientific research (generation of knowledge);
education, including personal development (transfer of knowledge and skills,
formation of competencies); innovation (application of knowledge).
The University is actively fostering an entrepreneurial culture. In this context,
extensive work is being carried out to engage staff, faculty, and students in
innovative activities in the fields of education, science, and society. To achieve this
goal, an entrepreneurship module has been incorporated into all educational
programs across disciplines and specializations, which helps to develop
entrepreneurial thinking and lay the foundations for entrepreneurial skills among
students.
Throughout the year, the University organizes innovation project
competitions (Turan Innovation, Start-up Turan) to support the implementation of
entrepreneurial ideas. The best projects also participate in competitions organized
by the Akimat of Almaty, the Atameken National Chamber of Entrepreneurs, the
Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan (MSHE
RK), as well as in international contests. The most promising projects receive
University grants for the creation of startups. Some student projects are supervised
by faculty founders, who share their own entrepreneurial experience, while others
are implemented independently by students.
The University “Turan”, as a representative of the International Triple Helix
Association (USA, chaired by H. Etzkowitz), promotes innovation, scientific
knowledge, and practical collaboration between the university, business, and
government–the core idea of the Triple Helix model.
Expanding the boundaries and opportunities for students, Turan University
has become a member of the International Movement for Leisure Activities in
Science and Technology (MILSET-Vostok) in collaboration with Russian partners,
with the aim of promoting scientific and technical creativity among young people.
To ensure the best conditions for developing an entrepreneurial spirit and
supporting the success of young entrepreneurs, Turan University pays great attention
to involving students in the global youth entrepreneurship movement ENACTUS.
The business incubator of the University provides acceleration and development
support for student projects. One of the main areas of youth entrepreneurship at the
University is the activity of the Public Association “TURAN JASTARY”, founded
by students and master’s students, which implements social entrepreneurship
projects.
To enhance the innovative potential and competitiveness of students, the
Summer | and | Winter | Schools | of | Entrepreneurship | “TURAN
ENTREPRENEURSHIP” operate at the University. The organizers of these schools
are Turan University, the Kazakhstan Marketing Association, and the Tau-Turan
Educational and Wellness Complex. The goal of the schools is to promote
entrepreneurial activity as an effective life strategy. Access to all Innovative
Entrepreneurial University structures and to all events organized by them is open to
everyone interested.
Collaboration with leading innovative companies of the country provides the
University with excellent opportunities for development in line with its strategic
direction. For example, cooperation with «Almaty Hub by Astana Hub» (part of the
Astana Hub International IT Startup Technopark Corporate Fund) contributes to the
formation and development of the University's innovative and support for
entrepreneurship, as well as the implementation of digital solutions.
In line with the innovative and entrepreneurial orientation of the University,
many students, master’s, and doctoral students launch their own businesses with the
informational and financial support of the University. The Corporate Fund “Business
Angels of Turan University” has been established. The number of young
entrepreneurs is growing every year.
The dynamics of students’ entrepreneurial spirit are monitored within the
framework of the international project GUESSS, in which Turan University is a
participant.
The innovative and entrepreneurial development of the University has already
produced tangible results. The Catalogue of Innovative Products of Turan University
includes: innovative projects and developments of faculty and students; the use of
innovative equipment and technologies in the educational process; publications of
the teaching staff; the launch of new innovative educational programs; innovative
methods and techniques of teaching.
The results of the faculty’s innovative activities are confirmed by copyright
certificates and patents.
5. Selection of the Educational Trajectory. Procedure for Student
Registration
5.1 A student independently designs an individual learning trajectory in the
form of an Individual Study Plan (ISP) based on the educational program (EP) and
the Course Catalogue (as part of the Modular Educational Program – MEP). The
student selects the required number of modules amounting to no fewer than 20 and
no more than 40 ECTS credits in the main semesters, including all types of academic
activities – theoretical training, internship, research/experimental research work,
and forms of final assessment – within the Mandatory Component (MC),
University Component (UC), and Elective Component (EC). In an additional
semester, students may take up to 15 credits.
Afterward, in accordance with the State Compulsory Educational Standard
(SCES), the educational program (EP), and students’ individual study plans, a
working curriculum for the educational program for the academic year is developed.
The working curriculum is approved by a decision of the Academic Council upon
the recommendation of the Academic Board. The student bears personal
responsibility for compiling the Individual Study Plan and for the full completion of
the course of study in accordance with the requirements of the working curriculum
for the respective specialization.
5.2 Individual academic planning is carried out for the academic year under the
supervision of advisors. For master’s and doctoral students, individual academic
planning is conducted with the participation of their scientific supervisors
(consultants).
5.3 When developing their Individual Study Plan (ISP), students must become
familiar with the Regulations on the Organization of the Educational Process under
the Credit System of Learning and the Rules for the Organization of the Educational
Process; comply with the established registration deadlines for academic disciplines
and for making changes to the ISP; and register for no fewer than the required
number of credits per academic year in order to complete the educational program
of the corresponding level.
Students studying on a tuition-paying basis, depending on their financial
capacity, form of study, and individual abilities, may compile their ISP with a
smaller number of credits than prescribed for the educational program of the
corresponding level (but no fewer than 20 credits, except for students repeating a
course, reinstated, or transferred from other universities). In such cases, the duration
of study is extended accordingly.
5.4 The selection of modules (courses) shall be carried out with mandatory
consideration of the sequence of course study and the completion of prerequisites.
The Director of the respective Graduate School and advisors are responsible for
timely informing students with academic deficiencies about the possibilities of
making them up in subsequent periods of theoretical study, without violating the
prerequisite requirements for course completion.
5.5 Information about the academic disciplines, including their brief
descriptions, is provided in the Course Catalogue (as part of the Modular
Educational Program – MEP). The catalogue is communicated to students by
advisors of the respective Graduate Schools, the Director of the Graduate School of
Business (GSB), and is also published as part of the MEP on the University’s official
website and in the Automated Management System “Turan” (AMS “Turan”).
Student registration for course enrollment is organized by the Registrar’s Office. For
the purpose of carrying out organizational, methodological, and advisory work,
structural units and advisors (scientific supervisors/consultants) are involved.
Registration is conducted through the AMS “Turan” system (https://portal.turanedu.kz). The registration deadlines are specified in the academic calendar.
5.6 During the adaptation week (which is not included in the duration of the
first semester), newly admitted students are introduced to the rules for organizing
the educational process based on the credit technology of education, the Academic
Policy of Turan University, the educational programs, the academic calendar, the
class schedule, the internal regulations, and the rules of residence in the dormitory.
Students are also introduced to the Student Code, which consists of the General Code
of Conduct, the Academic Code of Student Conduct, the Anti-Discrimination and
Anti-Harassment Policy, and the Complaint and Appeal Policy and Procedure.
During this week, first-year students meet with the University administration, get
acquainted with University services, receive a unified login and password to access
LMS Canvas, AMS “Turan”, and the University electronic library. If necessary, they
undergo testing for the recognition of formal and non-formal learning results to
determine the correspondence of their knowledge to the required learning outcomes
of Level 6 in relevant disciplines. The learning results are recognized if the test
score is 50 points or higher. In addition, students register for modules (courses) in
the AMS “Turan” system and receive a Student Guidebook. The basis for
registration is the Order of Admission and, for students studying on a fee-paying
basis, the payment of the first installment (20% of the total tuition fee for one
academic year). Continuing students register for modules (courses) of the next
academic year within the period specified in the academic calendar. Course retake
(Retake) is conducted during the same registration period but requires prepayment,
as the repeated study of courses is conducted on a fee-paying basis for all educational
programs and forms of study.
5.6(1) During the period of restrictive measures related to the spread of
coronavirus infection, the adaptation week is conducted remotely and in accordance
with the provisions of Clause 5.6.
5.7 Registration for the summer semester (except for the final year of study) is
carried out after the completion of the spring semester.
5.8 Students who have completed all prerequisites for the selected modules
(courses), have no outstanding financial obligations, and have undergone a
comprehensive medical examination (once per academic year) are eligible for
registration.
5.9 Course syllabi and instructional materials (CSIM) included in the working
curriculum must be uploaded to the LMS Canvas no later than five (5) working days
before the beginning of the semester in which these courses are offered. After the
specified period, access to upload CSIM materials will be automatically closed.
5.10 The Individual Study Plan (ISP) determines the individual educational
trajectory of each student. The ISP is automatically generated in the Turan
Information System based on the results of course enrollment according to the
Modular Educational Program (MEP).
The student’s ISP, generated in the Turan Information System, must be printed,
signed by the student and the advisor, agreed upon by the Director of the Graduate
School, and approved by the Dean of the Faculty. The approved ISP is then
submitted to the Registrar’s Office for the organization of the interim assessment.
The deadline for submitting the ISP is the first week of the academic year.
Course registration takes place during the period specified in the academic
calendar. Students may make changes to their ISP within one week from the
beginning of the semester.
5.11 The Individual Study Plans (ISP) of students enrolled in Academic
Mobility (AM) programs are subject to coordination with the partner university. For
this purpose, the graduating Graduate School, based on the course syllabi,
determines the equivalence of courses offered by the partner university to the
Typical Curriculum (TC) and the Educational Program (EP) of the Innovative
Entrepreneurial University “Turan” for the corresponding major.
5.12 The class schedule is formed based on students’ registration for modules
(courses). Academic streams and groups are organized according to the principle of
having a sufficient number of students registered for a given course and instructor,
ensuring the economic feasibility of instruction.
The student capacity of academic streams and groups at the Innovative
Entrepreneurial University “Turan” is as follows:
- Lectures – 60 to 90 students;
- Practical (seminar) classes – 20 to 30 students;
- Language and laboratory classes – no more than 15 students.
For disciplines in programs with a small student contingent, academic streams
are formed on the principle of enrolling all students registered for the discipline.
If the number of students registered for a discipline is below the minimum
established threshold, the discipline is not opened. Within five days after the end of
the registration period, the advisor informs the students who have registered for the
discipline and requests them to reselect other disciplines.
5.12(1) During restrictive measures related to the spread of coronavirus
infection, the educational process is organized in blended and distance learning
formats. Academic streams, groups, and class schedules for in-person instruction are
formed in compliance with the Sanitary and Epidemiological Regulations (SER)
(including maintaining a 2-meter social distance, among other requirements). The
transition to blended and/or distance learning formats is regulated by the
recommendations and decisions of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of
the Republic of Kazakhstan (MSHE RK).
5.13 At the Turan University, distance learning technologies are applied.
Students have the right to study individual courses remotely, in accordance
with the procedure established by the authorized body in the field of education.
6. Independent Work of Students
6.1 Under the credit-based learning system, Independent Work of Students
(IWS) is divided into two parts: independent work carried out under the guidance of
a teacher (IWST – undergraduate level, IWMT – master’s level, IWDT – doctoral
level), and independent work completed entirely on one’s own (IWS, IWM, IWD –
actual independent work).
6.2 The entire volume of Independent Work (IW) is verified through
assignments that require students to engage in daily independent study.
6.3 Independent work under the guidance of a teacher is a non-classroom
learning activity carried out by students in direct contact with the instructor. This
type of work (IWST / IWMT / IWDT) is performed according to a separate schedule,
which is not included in the general class timetable.
The scope of IWST/IWMT/IWDT includes: consultations on the most
complex topics of the curriculum; supervision of homework assignments;
monitoring of semester papers, reports, and other types of IW tasks.
6.4 Descriptions of all types of Independent Work (IW) must be provided in
the CSIM (Course Syllabus and Instructional Materials), including the schedule for
assignment submission.
6.5 A student has the right not to attend Independent Work under the
Teacher’s supervision (IWST/IWMT/IWDT) sessions if the completion of
independent | tasks | does | not | present | any | difficulties.
However, the instructor’s presence in the classroom according to the
IWST/IWMT/IWDT schedule is mandatory.
6.6 During restrictive measures related to the spread of coronavirus infection,
the Independent Work of students is organized in compliance with the Sanitary and
Epidemiological Regulations (SER).
7. Research / Experimental Research Work of Students (RW / ERW)
7.1 Research Work of Students (RWS) is a mandatory and integral part of the
training of qualified specialists at the university, representing an inseparable
component of a unified process that combines educational, developmental, and
scientific-innovative activities. The Academic Council oversees the organization
and implementation of RWS.
Responsibility for student research lies with the graduating Graduate School,
the academic supervisor (instructor), and the faculty member in charge of RWS.
7.2 To ensure a systematic approach to addressing these issues, three main
types of organization of Research Work of Students (RWS) have been identified.
1) Research Work (RW) integrated into the educational process, the main
objective of which is to enhance the learning process according to the principle: the
higher the level of study, the greater the degree of independent work. The content of
this type of research activity includes the study of academic literature, preparation
of essays, reports, and projects containing research components. Directors of
Graduate Schools are responsible for ensuring that students complete the RW
included in the academic curricula.
2) Research Activities Complementing the Educational Process. The main
objective of this type of research is to go beyond the standard curriculum,
individualize the learning process, and create conditions for ensuring continuity of
education at the master’s and doctoral levels. The content of research work at this
stage includes academic competitions, contests, scientific conferences, seminars,
and round-table discussions. To organize this type of RWS, creative groups are
established by order of the Rector, under the supervision of a research advisor.
The research advisor forms a creative group consisting of students who have
expressed a desire to engage in applied, methodological, and exploratory scientific
research.
3) Scientific research conducted in parallel with the educational process. The
main objective is the scientific professionalization of students under the supervision
of highly qualified faculty members and researchers, which includes specialization,
preparation for a specific field of scientific activity, and the selection of a research
supervisor. The content of work at this stage involves participation in planned
scientific research carried out under grants, government-funded and contractual
projects, as well as the commercialization of research results.
7.3 An integral component of the educational programs of master's and doctoral
studies is research (experimental research) work, which includes the preparation of
scientific publications based on research results and the completion of a master's or
doctoral dissertation. Master’s students enrolled in professional programs complete
a master’s project.
7.4 Research (experimental research) work is carried out under the supervision
of an academic advisor or consultant, who is approved by rector’s order within two
months after the enrolment of a master’s or doctoral student, based on the decision
of the Academic Council.
The academic advisor/consultant of a master’s or doctoral student must hold an
academic degree and be actively engaged in scientific research in the relevant field
of study, having scientific publications in recognized journals with a non-zero
impact factor.
7.5 The research (experimental research) work of students must be reflected in
the individual study plan for the entire period of study, indicating the topic, research
direction, timelines, and forms of reporting. The individual plan must also include
plans for internships, preparation of the master’s dissertation, scientific publications,
and research placements.
The individual work plan of a master’s or doctoral student is developed under
the supervision of the academic advisor/consultant. Academic advisors/consultants
are responsible for the timely and high-quality completion by students of all types
of work provided for in the individual work plan.
7.6 The research (experimental research) work of master’s and doctoral
students is distributed across the semesters of study, indicating the number of credits
assigned in each semester. At the end of each academic period, the master’s/doctoral
student presents a report at a meeting of the respective graduate school in the
presence of the academic advisor/consultant, and the results of the research are
evaluated on a 100-point scale.
7.7 A student who has completed the full theoretical course of the postgraduate
educational program but has not fulfilled the research component is granted the
opportunity to recomplete the credits of the research component and defend the
dissertation/project in the following academic year on a paid basis.
7.8 As part of the research (experimental research) work, the individual work
plan of a master's or doctoral student provides for the mandatory completion of a
research internship in scientific organizations and/or organizations of relevant
industries or fields of activity – including, for doctoral students, abroad – in order
to become acquainted with innovative technologies and new types of production.
The internship program is developed in accordance with the field of study and the
individual work plan for the entire period of study. The funding standards for
international internships are established by the relevant regulatory documents of the
Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan (MSHE
RK).
7.9 During the period of restrictive measures related to the spread of
coronavirus infection, research work of students is carried out according to their
individual work plans in distance and blended learning formats, in accordance with
the academic calendar, the relevant Rector’s order, and in compliance with the
Sanitary and Epidemiological Regulations (SER). International research internships
are organized in accordance with the methodological guidelines of the Ministry of
Science and Higher Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan (MSHE RK).
8. Assessment of Students’ Academic Performance
8.1 The following forms of assessment are provided to monitor students’
academic performance: current, midterm, and final assessment.
8.2 Current assessment is a systematic evaluation of students’ knowledge in
accordance with the course syllabus, conducted by the instructor during both in-class
and out-of-class sessions throughout the academic period.
8.3 Midterm assessment is the evaluation of students’ academic achievements
upon completion of a section (module) of a course. For courses worth 3 or more
credits, the midterm assessment is conducted during the 8th and 15th weeks of the
semester and graded on a 100-point scale for each. For 2-credit courses, the midterm
assessment is conducted during the 10th week and also graded on a 100-point scale.
When assigning a midterm grade, the results of current assessment are taken into
account. Midterm results must be entered into the record sheet in the Turan AIS
system no later than the Monday following the assessment week and are included in
the calculation of the final grade. Revising midterm results to improve grades is not
permitted.
8.4 The form of current and midterm assessments is determined by the
instructor, depending on the specifics of the course, and is approved by the
Academic Board as part of the Course Syllabus (CSIM).
Current and midterm assessments may take the form of tests, written
assignments, colloquia, essays, case studies, reports, or evaluations of students’
participation in business games, round tables, and similar activities. Information
about the forms of current and midterm assessments is included in the course
syllabus and communicated to students during the first class session. Students who
achieve at least 50 points in the midterm assessment are admitted to the final
assessment.
8.5 If a student, for valid reasons supported by official documentation, fails to
earn points for the midterm assessments, they are granted permission to retake the
midterm within the period specified in the Dean’s directive.
8.6 The final assessment is conducted to evaluate the learning outcomes of
students and to determine the quality of their mastery of the course program. It is
held during the interim assessment period in the form of an exam. If a course extends
over several academic periods, the final assessment may be conducted for the portion
of the course studied in the current period.
8.7 Students are admitted to the interim assessment if their average score from
midterm assessments is at least 50 points. When determining the final grade for a
course, both the exam score and the average midterm score earned during the
academic period are taken into account according to the following formula: Final
Grade (%) = 0.6 × (M1% + M2%) / 2 + 0.4 × E%, where M1 and M2 are the midterm
results | and | E | is | the | exam | result.
The exam is graded on a 100-point scale.
8.8 In order to enhance the quality of the educational program implementation
and ensure the objectivity of student assessment, the processes of instruction and
final evaluation are separated.
Interim and final assessments are organized by the Registrar’s Office.
8.8.1 The final assessment of students’ academic achievements in completed
courses is conducted either in the form of computer-based testing or an oral
examination. The examiner is the instructor of the course. Proctoring is used to
conduct the final assessment.
In the case of final assessment conducted in the form of computer-based
testing in the university’s computer lab, proctoring functions are performed by
staff of the Registrar’s Office. The examiner-instructor must be present during the
computer-based testing.
If the final assessment is conducted as an oral examination, proctoring
functions are carried out by staff of the Dean’s Office or the Graduate Schools,
appointed by Dean’s order upon recommendation of the Director of the Graduate
School.
Faculty members of the Graduate Schools perform proctoring in accordance
with the approved schedule of in-office hours during the exam period, compiled
according to the annual faculty presence schedules in the offices of the Graduate
Schools. To ensure independence and objectivity, the examiner and proctor must not
be employees of the same unit.
A valid identity document must be presented as a mandatory requirement for
proctoring.
8.8.2 In the case of final assessment conducted as online computer-based
testing (only for courses approved for distance learning), the auto-proctoring system
“OES” supported by LMS Canvas is used. This system allows for real-time student
verification, monitoring of behavior, and video recording of the examination.
Presenting a valid identity document before the start of testing is a mandatory
requirement for auto-proctoring.

9.2 The application of new or revised criteria and methods for assessing

9.3 The adopted criteria and methods for assessing students’ knowledge cannot

9.4 The applied assessment criteria and methods must be reflected in the course

9.5 Records of assessment results are maintained through examination
registers, including those automatically generated in the ASU “Turan” system.
9.6 After the completion of the examination for each course, a final grade is
assigned to the student, which serves as an assessment of their academic
achievements. A passing grade is recorded in the examination register for the course,
indicating the credits earned.
When conducting interim assessment for a course, both the exam grade and the
average grade from ongoing performance assessment during the academic period are
taken into account. The ongoing performance assessment (rating for eligibility)
accounts for 60% of the final course grade, while the exam grade accounts for 40%
of the final course grade.
9.7 A student who disagrees with the final assessment results must file an
appeal no later than the next working day after the exam. The appeal will only be
considered if the student indicates in the appeal that the question is inappropriate or
inconsistent with the course curriculum.
During the examination period (interim assessment), by order of the Rector, an
appeal commission is established from among the teaching staff whose
qualifications correspond to the profile of the appealed disciplines. The decision of
the appeal commission is formalized in a protocol, on the basis of which an
examination record sheet is prepared.
In the event that, as a result of the appeal, the grade received is lower than the
one the student obtained on the exam, the final grade is recorded in the transcript
and counted towards the GPA. The appeal results are entered by the Registrar’s
Office after the decision of the appeal commission has been approved by order.
9.8 In the case of receiving an “unsatisfactory” grade corresponding to the mark
“FX,” the student has the opportunity to retake the final assessment without
repeating the course/module program, on a paid basis.
In the case of receiving an “unsatisfactory” grade corresponding to the mark
“F,” the student is re-enrolled (Retake) in the given course/module, attends all types
of classes, completes all types of academic work in accordance with the program,
and retakes the final assessment.
In this case, the student must undergo the course registration procedure again.
Re-taking a course (Retake) is carried out only on a paid basis. After three weeks
from the start of classes, a student taking a Retake is not allowed to withdraw from
the course; otherwise, a grade of “F” is assigned for the course. The payment made
for the course is non-refundable. If a student receives an unsatisfactory grade in an
elective course, they have the right to replace it with another course from the same
block, subject to approval by the advisor and the Registrar’s Office.
9.9 If a student misses an exam for a valid reason supported by official
documentation (such as illness with a medical certificate confirmed by the head of
the university’s medical and wellness center, etc.), in order to be admitted to the
exam, the student must submit an application to the Vice-Rector for Academic
Affairs for consideration. The application must be signed by the Dean of the faculty
and the Director of the Department for Academic Affairs, state the reasons for
missing the exam, and include the originals of supporting documents (certificates).
The application must be submitted no later than 2 working days from the date of the
exam. If the reason for missing the exam is recognized as valid, an individual exam
schedule is set for the student. Upon successful completion of the exam, the final
grade is recalculated and recorded in the examination record sheet and the transcript.
9.9.1 During the period of restrictive measures related to the spread of the
coronavirus infection, a student who “did not appear” for an online exam for a valid
reason must send an electronic application by corporate email addressed to the Dean
of the faculty in free form and provide confirmation of the validity of the reason.
With the Dean’s permission, the student takes the exam on another exam day. The
date of the rescheduled exam is determined as follows: for oral and creative exams,
as well as take-home open book exams – by the Graduate School; for online testing
– by the Department for Academic Affairs (Registrar’s Office).
9.9.2 If a student does not have sufficient technical resources (a
computer/laptop equipped with a camera and microphone) or a stable internet
connection, they take the online exam on the university campus. The student sends
a free-form application by corporate email to their advisor or to the Student Service
Center (SSC). The Registrar’s Office sets the exam schedule for the student and
invites them to the campus to take the exam.
A student may be granted another time to take the online exam if a technical
malfunction occurred during the exam that made the assessment impossible. In this
case, the teacher prepares an electronic report of the technical malfunction in the
prescribed form and, on the same day, sends a copy of the report to the supervising
Vice-Rector, the Dean of the faculty, the Director of the Graduate School, and the
Registrar’s Office specialist. If the exam is administered by a commission, the
teacher ensures a corporate email distribution to all commission members.

9.19 Turan University recognizes non-formal education (of students and

Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan No. 544 of October 24, 2023, and the
Minister of School Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan No. 322 of October 24,
2023).
Mechanism for the Recognition of Non-Formal Education Outcomes:
1) the recognition of learning outcomes acquired through non-formal education
is permitted for educational components included in the educational program;
2) the recognition of learning outcomes is carried out in the semester in which
the educational component is scheduled to be taken according to the curriculum of
the specific educational program;
3) the recognition of learning outcomes acquired through non-formal education
applies to compulsory courses, university-wide courses, and elective educational
components, with the exception of the diploma project;
4) an educational component may be transferred only in its entirety;
5) the University may recognize learning outcomes acquired through nonformal education in an amount not exceeding 10% (24 credits) of the total number
of credits in a specific educational program.
The validation of learning outcomes acquired through non-formal education
must comply with the Rules for the recognition of learning outcomes obtained
through non-formal education in other institutions, as established by Turan
University.
10. Organization and completion of internship
10.1 Internship is a compulsory component of the professional educational
program of higher and postgraduate education.
The main objective of students’ professional internship is to consolidate and
deepen the theoretical knowledge acquired during the course of study, as well as to
gain practical experience and skills in the chosen field of study.
Professional internship at Turan University is divided:
- for bachelor’s degree programs – into educational, industrial, pedagogical,
and pre-graduation internships;
- for master’s degree programs – into research and pedagogical internships (for
the scientific and pedagogical track), and industrial internships (for the professional
track, MBA, and EMBA);
- for doctoral programs – into research and pedagogical internships.
10.2 Each type of internship has its own objectives, tasks, and program. The
program is developed by the Graduate School, coordinated with enterprises
(institutions, organizations) designated as internship bases, reviewed, and approved
at the Graduate School meeting. In terms of content, the program reflects the profile
of the specialty, the specifics of the type of internship, the requirements of
professional standards and the educational program, as well as the nature of the
organization’s activities, technological process, and internship site.
The professional internship program is periodically updated and reflects
modern advances in science and technology, as well as innovative technologies
applied at the internship base.
10.3 The duration of the internship and the number of credits are determined in
accordance with the working curriculum and the academic calendar of the respective
specialty. At Turan University, each academic year concludes with an internship,
except for the final year of study.
Students include the corresponding type of internship in their individual study
plan.
Upon completion of each type of professional internship, students submit a
report to the respective Graduate School. The report is reviewed by the supervisor
and defended before the internship supervisor or a committee consisting of members
of the School. The results of the report defense are assessed using the point-rating
letter grading system (Table 1) and are taken into account in the calculation of the
GPA. The overall results of the internship are summarized at the Academic Board
of the University.
10.4 Students admitted to the university under shortened educational programs
and working in their field of study have the right to undergo professional internship
at their workplace.
In this case, a tripartite agreement on the professional internship with the
organization is not concluded.
The student’s employment is confirmed by a certificate from the workplace and
a detailed reference describing their professional work activities.
10.5 Educational internships may be conducted within the university’s
departments. Industrial, pedagogical, and pre-graduation internships are conducted
in organizations whose statutory activities correspond to the field of specialist
training and the requirements of the educational program, and which have qualified
staff to supervise the professional internship as well as the necessary material and
technical resources.
10.6 The educational program of the scientific and pedagogical master’s degree
includes two types of internships:
1) pedagogical internship – in an educational institution;
2) research internship – at the place where the dissertation is conducted.
The pedagogical internship is conducted with the aim of developing practical
teaching skills and methodology. The pedagogical internship may be carried out
during the period of theoretical training without interrupting the educational process.
In this case, master’s students may be involved in conducting classes at the
bachelor’s level.
The research internship of a master’s student is conducted with the aim of
familiarizing them with the latest theoretical, methodological, and technological
advances in domestic and international science, as well as modern methods of
scientific research, processing, and interpretation of experimental data.
10.7 The educational program of the professional master’s degree and MBA
includes an industrial internship.
The industrial internship of a master’s student is conducted with the aim of
consolidating the theoretical knowledge acquired during the course of study, gaining
practical skills, competencies, and professional experience in the chosen specialty,
as well as mastering best practices.
10.8 The content of the research/industrial internship is determined by the topic
of the dissertation (project) research. At the end of each internship period, the student
prepares a report summarizing the results of the internship.
10.9 The doctoral educational program must include pedagogical and research
internships for students in the Doctor of Philosophy program, and a research
internship for students in the DBA program.
The pedagogical internship may be conducted during the period of theoretical
training without interrupting the educational process; in this case, doctoral students
may be involved in conducting classes at the bachelor’s and master’s levels.
The research internship of a doctoral student is conducted with the aim of
studying the latest theoretical, methodological, and technological advances in
domestic and international science, as well as consolidating practical skills, applying
modern methods of scientific research, processing, and interpreting experimental
data in the dissertation research.
The content of the research internship is determined by the topic of the doctoral
dissertation. At the end of each internship period, the doctoral student prepares a
report summarizing the results of the internship.
10.10 The duration of the internship and the number of credits for master’s and
doctoral programs are determined in accordance with the working curriculum and
the academic calendar of the respective specialty.
10.11 Master’s and doctoral students submit a report to the respective Graduate
School, which is reviewed by the supervisor and defended before a committee
appointed by the Director of the Graduate School. The results of the report defense
are assessed using the point-rating letter grading system (Table 1) and are taken into
account in the calculation of the GPA. The overall results of the internship are
summarized at the Academic Board of the University.
10.12 During the period of restrictive measures related to the spread of the
coronavirus infection, all types of internships are conducted in blended/remote
formats in accordance with students’ individual plans, the academic calendar, and
the relevant orders of the Rector specifying the format of the internship.
11. Organization and conduct of the state exam in the course "History of
Kazakhstan"
11.1 Students of all bachelor’s degree programs take the state exam in the
course “History of Kazakhstan” upon completion of the course, within the same
academic period according to the academic calendar.
11.2 The organization of the state exam is carried out by the School conducting
the course “History of Kazakhstan” in cooperation with the Faculty Dean’s Office
and the Registrar’s Office.
11.3 For the conduct of the state exam in the course “History of Kazakhstan”
the School, based on the standard curriculum for this course, develops a working
syllabus that is unified for all forms of study and specialties, which is approved by
the Academic Council of the University.
11.4 The format of the state exam in the course “History of Kazakhstan” is
determined by the Academic Council of the University.
11.5 For the administration of the state exam in the course “History of
Kazakhstan,” based on the recommendation of the Faculty Dean, a State
Examination Commission (hereinafter referred to as the SEC) is formed, consisting
of the Chair of the SEC and its members for the calendar year.
11.6 The Chair and members of the State Examination Commission for the
course “History of Kazakhstan” are approved by order of the Rector based on the
decision of the Academic Council.
11.7 The schedule of the State Examination Commission (SEC) meetings is
prepared by the Registrar’s Office in accordance with the academic calendar and
approved by the Rector no later than two weeks before the start of the state exam.
The duration of an SEC meeting shall not exceed six academic hours per day.
11.8 The results of the state exam are assessed using the point-rating letter
grading system for evaluating students’ academic achievements, with their
conversion into the traditional grading scale. The final grade is assigned taking into
account both the admission rating grade and the state exam grade. The admission
rating grade for the state exam constitutes at least 30% of the final grade for the
course.
11.9 If a student receives an “unsatisfactory” grade in the state exam for the
course “History of Kazakhstan,” they must, on a paid basis, re-enroll in this course
in the next academic period or summer semester, attend all types of classes again,
meet the requirements of ongoing assessment, obtain admission, and retake the state
exam.
11.10 The retake of a positive grade on the state examination in the course
‘History of Kazakhstan’ for the purpose of improving it shall be carried out
according to the procedure specified in clause 9.8 of this Policy.
11.11 A student who does not agree with the result of the state examination
shall submit an appeal no later than the day following the holding of the State
Examination Commission. For the purpose of considering the appeal, an appeal
commission for the course ‘History of Kazakhstan’ is established by the Rector's
order, consisting of experienced instructors in this discipline.
11.12 The results of the state examination in the course ‘History of Kazakhstan’
are taken into account when summing up the results of the examination session in
which it is scheduled.
11.13 Upon completion of the state examination, the chair of the State
Examination Commission prepares a report on the work of the Commission, which
is discussed and approved at a meeting of the University Academic Council.
11.14 During the period of restrictive measures related to the spread of the
coronavirus infection, the state examination is conducted online in accordance with
paragraph 3.1 ‘Procedure for conducting the state examination in the course ‘History
of Kazakhstan’ using distance learning technologies at the University’ of the
Regulations on the procedure for conducting ongoing academic performance
monitoring, midterm and final assessment of students at Turan University.
12. Final Attestation of Students in Bachelor’s Degree Programs
12.1 The final attestation of undergraduate students at higher education
institutions is carried out in the forms established by the State Educational Standards,
within the timeframes specified in the academic calendar and the approved curricula
of the respective programs.
12.2 The final attestation of undergraduate students (except for students of
educational programs in the field of ‘Arts’) is conducted in two forms: 1) the
preparation and defense of a bachelor’s thesis or graduation project; 2) the
completion of a comprehensive examination in the educational program. A student
submits an application addressed to the Rector of the University, indicating the
chosen form of final attestation (in the case of selecting the first form, the topic of
the thesis/project must also be specified). The number of students defending a thesis
or graduation project shall not exceed 50% of the total number of final-year students.
Priority is given to the student with the highest GPA.
A student applying for an honors degree is required to choose the form
‘preparation and defense of a bachelor’s thesis or graduation project.
For the final attestation of students in educational programs in the field of
‘Arts,’ a comprehensive examination is not provided. The form of final attestation
is the completion and defense of a creative graduation project (a graduation film for
the educational programs ‘Directing,’ ‘Cinematography,’ ‘Film and Television
Producing,’ or a graduation performance for the educational program ‘Acting’).
The comprehensive examination program reflects integrated knowledge and
key competencies that meet the requirements of the labor market in accordance with
the educational program of higher education.
12.3 For the purpose of conducting the final attestation of students, an
Attestation Commission (hereinafter – AC) shall be established for individual
specialties or groups of specialties in higher education. By November 10 of the
current year, Faculty Deans shall submit to the Department of Academic Affairs
nominations for chairs of the AC from among professors, associate professors,
scholars, instructors, and experienced industry specialists with practical experience
relevant to the profile of graduating bachelors, who are not employed by the
University. The chair and members of the AC shall be approved by order of the
Rector on the basis of a decision of the University Academic Council no later than
January 10 of the current academic year, and shall serve for the duration of the
current calendar year. The composition of the AC shall be formed from professors,
associate professors, and highly qualified specialists corresponding to the profile of
the graduating students. The number of members of the AC shall be determined by
the University.
12.3.1 Changes in the composition of the AC during the academic year shall be
approved by the corresponding order of the Rector, issued on the basis of a
submission by the Faculty Dean and the prior agreement of the candidate with the
Vice-Rector for Academic Affairs. In order to replace the chair of the AC, the
candidate shall be submitted for consideration by the Academic Council, and in the
event of approval, shall be confirmed by order of the Rector.
12.4 Students who have fully completed the educational process in accordance
with the requirements of the State Educational Standards, the educational program,
the curriculum, the individual study plan, and the course syllabi are admitted to the
final attestation. Admission of students to the final attestation is formalized by an
order of the Faculty Dean in the form of a list indicating the students’ surnames, first
names, patronymics (if applicable), and specialties (educational programs), no later
than two weeks before the start of the final attestation.
Admission to the final assessment for students who have earned additional
credits is carried out in accordance with the Schedule for Taking Exams and the
Schedule for Comprehensive Exams and Thesis (Project) Defenses, which are
approved by the Vice Rector for Academic Affairs.
A final-year student who has not fulfilled the requirements of the educational
program, the curriculum, the individual study plan, and the course syllabi shall
remain for a repeated year of study without attending the summer semester.
12.5 By October 10 of the current academic year, the university submits
applications (in free form) regarding the expected graduation to the Banknote
Factory of the National Bank of the Republic of Kazakhstan for the purpose of
forming a request for educational documents (diplomas and transcripts).
12.6 The schedule of the AC’s work is prepared by the Registrar’s Office,
approved by the Rector, and communicated publicly no later than two weeks before
the start of the AC’s activities.
12.7 In the case of choosing the first form of final attestation (clause 12.2), the
supervisor and the topic of the bachelor’s thesis/project are approved by order of the
Rector for each student on the basis of a decision of the University Academic
Council at the beginning of the final year of study (no later than December 1 of the
current year).
12.7.1 In the case of a change to the approved form of final attestation, an
appropriate order of the Rector shall be issued on the basis of a submission by the
Faculty Dean. The proposed changes are submitted for discussion by the Faculty
Council and agreed upon with the Vice-Rector for Academic Affairs.
12.8 A student has the right to choose the supervisor and the field of research
for the bachelor’s thesis/project.
12.9 The comprehensive examination in the specialty is conducted according
to a program developed by the University on the basis of course syllabi. The program
of the comprehensive examination in the specialty is approved by a decision of the
University Academic Council no later than one month before the examination.
12.10 The duration of an AC meeting shall not exceed six (6) academic hours
per day.
12.11 The defense of a bachelor’s thesis/project includes the preparation of the
thesis/project and the defense procedure. The thesis/project is aimed at identifying
and evaluating the graduate’s analytical and research abilities. Theses/projects are
checked by the University for plagiarism. The defense of the bachelor’s
thesis/project is conducted at an open meeting of the AC.
Creative graduation projects of students in educational programs in the field of
Arts are not written works and are not subject to plagiarism checks.
12.12 A student may defend the bachelor’s thesis/project only if a positive
review has been provided by the supervisor. In the event that the supervisor issues a
negative conclusion of ‘not allowed to proceed to the defense,’ the student is not
permitted to defend the bachelor’s thesis/project.
12.13 Based on the results of the final attestation, grades are assigned according
to the point-rating system of student knowledge assessment, taking into account the
level of the student’s theoretical and practical preparation, as well as the supervisor’s
review of the bachelor’s thesis/project.
12.14 The results of the defense of the bachelor’s thesis (project) or the
comprehensive examinations are announced on the day of their conduct, after the
signing of the minutes of the AC meeting.
12.15 All meetings of the AC are documented by minutes. The minutes of AC
meetings are maintained individually for each graduate. The minutes are completed
by the AC secretary, who is approved as part of the commission and does not have
voting rights.
12.16 Decisions on the final attestation, as well as on the awarding of a degree
or qualification and the issuance of a bachelor’s diploma (with or without honors),
are made by the AC at a closed meeting through open voting by a simple majority
of the AC members participating in the meeting. In the event of a tie, the Chair’s
vote is decisive.
12.17 The minutes of AC meetings are kept in the university archive in
accordance with the requirements of the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan of
December 22, 1998, On the National Archival Fund and Archives.
12.18 A student who did not appear for the final attestation for a valid reason
shall submit an application in free form addressed to the Chair of the AC, provide a
document confirming the valid reason, and, with the Chair’s permission, take the
comprehensive examinations or defend the bachelor’s thesis (project) on another day
of the AC meeting.
12.19 A student who does not agree with the results of the final attestation shall
submit an appeal no later than the next working day after its conduct. For the purpose
of considering the appeal, an appeals committee is established by order of the Rector
from among experienced instructors whose qualifications correspond to the profile
of the specialty.
12.20 Medical documents submitted to the AC after the receipt of an
unsatisfactory grade are not considered.
12.21 A repeat defense of the diploma thesis (project) or a retake of the
comprehensive examinations for the purpose of improving a passing grade is not
permitted.
12.22 A repeat defense of the diploma thesis (project), as well as a retake of the
comprehensive examinations by students who received a grade of ‘unsatisfactory,’
is not allowed during the current final attestation period.
12.23 If, during the defense of the bachelor’s thesis (project), the thesis is
deemed unsatisfactory, the AC shall determine the possibility of resubmitting the
same thesis/project with revisions or developing a new thesis/project with a different
topic in the following academic year. This decision of the AC is recorded in the
minutes of the meeting.
12.24 A student who has not passed the final attestation shall be expelled from
the university by order of the rector as having not completed their studies.
12.25 A repeat final attestation for a student is conducted only in the forms for
which the student received an unsatisfactory grade in the previous final attestation.
The list of subjects included in the comprehensive examinations for students who
did not pass these exams is determined by the curriculum that was in effect in the
year the student completed the theoretical part of the program.
12.26 For a repeat final attestation, a student who was expelled from the
University no later than one month before the start of the final attestation of the
following academic year shall submit an application addressed to the Rector
requesting permission to take the repeat final attestation. Admission to the repeat
final attestation is formalized by an order of the Rector. Students are admitted to the
repeat final attestation only on a paid basis.
12.27 A student who has successfully completed the final attestation and
confirmed mastery of the corresponding higher education program shall, by decision
of the AC, be awarded the degree of ‘Bachelor’ or the qualification in the relevant
specialty, and issued a bachelor’s diploma with a transcript free of charge within five

14. Integral GPA (IGPA)
14.1 The University conducts a comprehensive assessment of a student’s
academic achievements by calculating the Integral Grade Point Average (IGPA) in
accordance with the Regulations on the Implementation of the Integral GPA at Turan
University, approved by the order of the Rector.
14.2 The IGPA is calculated at the end of each academic year.
14.3 The IGPA reflects the student’s overall level of academic achievement,
research aptitude, and the degree of development of social competencies.
14.4 The IGPA represents the student’s integrated academic performance and
is calculated as the sum of weighted components: 0.5 for academic achievements,
0.35 for research skills, and 0.15 for social competencies.
The IGPA is calculated using the following formula:
IGPA = 0.5 × GPA + 0.35 × iROS + 0.15 × SSCI, where:
GPA – the weighted average grade reflecting the level of the student’s
academic achievements;
iROS – the student’s research achievements;
SSCI – the student’s social and sports achievements.
14.3 Students independently (on a monthly basis) enter their personal research
achievements in the iROS Portfolio section and their social activities in the SSCI
Portfolio section of their profile in the university’s Automated Control System
(ACS), indicating the indicator, level/status, and attaching a supporting document or
hyperlink.
14.4 Verification of the data entered in the iROS Portfolio is carried out by the
teaching staff and the employees of the Research Department.
14.5 Verification of the data entered in the SSCI Portfolio is carried out by
academic advisors (curators) and the Head of the Youth Affairs Committee.

16. Kazakhstan Credit Transfer System Based on ECTS
16.1 The workload of one Kazakhstani credit of theoretical training, including
classroom instruction and students’ independent work, amounts to 30 hours.
16.2 To ensure academic mobility of students and recognition of educational
programs (at all levels and forms of higher and postgraduate education) within the
European Higher Education Area, it is necessary to convert Kazakhstani credits into
ECTS credits.
16.3 An academic credit under the ECTS is a unit of measurement of the total
workload required to study a course, including both classroom activities and
independent work. One ECTS credit corresponds to 25–30 academic hours.
16.4 The total academic workload, in addition to classroom instruction,
includes the following types of students’ independent learning activities: writing
essays, reports, and projects; completing laboratory work; preparing for various
types of current, midterm, and final assessments; collecting materials and writing a
thesis (project).
16.5 Under the ECTS framework, the total annual student workload equals 60
credits (30 credits per semester or 20 credits per trimester). The academic year lasts
30 weeks, with 6 additional weeks allocated for examinations (final assessment).
16.6 In European countries, the duration of studies required to obtain a
bachelor’s degree ranges from three to four years, requiring the completion of 180–
240 ECTS credits.
16.7 The workload of one Kazakhstani academic credit (30 academic hours)
corresponds to one ECTS credit (25–30 academic hours).
16.8 At Turan University, the ECTS coordinators are the directors of graduate
schools and educational programs of the Graduate School of TFA. ECTS
coordinators ensure compliance with the principles and mechanisms of ECTS and
provide guidance to students and faculty members on the practical and academic
aspects of ECTS implementation.
16.9 The ECTS grading scale includes five positive grades (“A” to “E”), the
grade “FX,” which can be corrected, and the grade “F,” which does not grant credits.
The Kazakhstani grading system, based on a point-rating letter system for
assessing students’ academic achievements, includes ten positive grades (“A” to “D–
”), which allow for the awarding of credits, as well as the grade “FX,” which may
be corrected, and the grade “F,” which does not grant credits.
16.10 The conversion of Kazakhstani credits into ECTS credits and the
conversion of grades into the point-rating letter grading system for assessing
students’ academic achievements are carried out in accordance with the established
appendices.
17. Academic Mobility of Students
17.1 In accordance with the University’s quality assurance policy, an
important objective of the University is to ensure academic mobility of students,
faculty members, and researchers. In this regard, the following tasks are defined:
− improving the quality of higher and postgraduate education;
− enhancing the effectiveness of scientific research;
− establishing and implementing external and internal integrative partnerships;
− increasing the competitiveness of specialists in the Kazakhstani labor market;
− developing intercultural communication skills and other competencies among
participants of academic mobility, which are essential for their successful
professional development.
17.2 The goals, objectives, and general rules for ensuring and implementing
academic mobility correspond to the fundamental principles of the Bologna Process.
Academic mobility of Turan University students is carried out within the framework
of a tripartite agreement/contract among the student, the sending university, and the
host university for participation in academic mobility programs. For outbound
(external) mobility, an invitation from the host university is required.
17.3 Academic mobility (AM) refers to the movement of students or teachingresearch staff for the purpose of study or conducting research for a specific academic
period – a semester or academic year – at another higher education institution
(within the country or abroad), with mandatory recognition and transfer of the
completed educational components in the form of credits at their home university,
or for continuation of studies at another institution.
17.4 Primarily, academic mobility of students and faculty members is ensured
through their participation in partner foreign universities for the purpose of:
− studying within joint educational programs or double-degree programs;
− participating in academic exchange programs;
− undertaking scientific and pedagogical internships;
− participating in summer schools;
− completing professional development courses;
− engaging in teaching and research activities.
17.5 External outbound academic mobility is implemented through
participation in competitions, partnership programs, internships, and other similar
activities, in accordance with the proposals of the Office for International
Cooperation (OIC) or on the initiative of students and faculty members themselves.
In the case of inbound external academic mobility, the organization of mobility for
foreign students involves the relevant graduate schools, the Department for
Academic Affairs, the Office of the Registrar, the Legal Counsel, and the Financial
and Economic Department.
17.6 Internal (domestic) inbound and outbound academic mobility is
coordinated by the graduate schools, the Department for Academic Affairs, the
dean’s offices, the Office of the Registrar, the Legal Counsel, and the Financial and
Economic Department. To implement internal inbound and outbound academic
mobility, a mobility agreement between Turan University and the partnering
Kazakhstani higher education institution is required.
17.7 Academic mobility of master’s and doctoral students may also be carried
out in the form of a scientific and practical internship.
17.8 If the academic mobility of students is financed through the travel grant
of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of Kazakhstan
(MSHE RK), a competition is announced. The procedure for selecting candidates is
carried out in accordance with the Regulations on the Competition Commission.
Participation in academic mobility within the framework of programs financed
at the state level is carried out in accordance with the Rules for Sending Students to
Study Abroad, including within the Framework of Academic Mobility, approved by
the Order of the Minister of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan
dated November 19, 2008, No. 613 (as amended).
17.9 The final document confirming studies under the mobility program is the
transcript, which is completed in the established form in English for students who
studied under the external academic mobility program, and in Russian and/or
Kazakh for students who studied under the internal academic mobility program. The
transcript includes information about the study program (course code), course title,
duration of study (year, semester, trimester), grade for the course (in the national
scale and in the ECTS scale), and the number of ECTS credits awarded. If necessary,
a student participating in the academic mobility program has the right to register for
the university’s courses to continue studying under their educational program using
distance learning, while the number of credits must not exceed 50% of the total
number of credits for the given academic period.
17.10 When arranging academic mobility for faculty members, it is necessary
to provide an official invitation from the host university, research institute,
conference organizers, or other relevant institutions. In cases where services are
rendered to the host institution on a paid basis (e.g., lecturing), an appropriate service
contract must be concluded. Upon receiving an official invitation, faculty members
and researchers planning an individual educational trajectory, academic, or research
program must coordinate all terms and dates of the mobility with the university
administration. Based on the application approved by the Rector, the Human
Resources Department issues an official order authorizing the assignment under the
academic mobility program.
17.11 Upon completion of academic mobility, a copy of the certificate
confirming participation in a conference, professional development courses, or other
relevant activities must be submitted to the Human Resources Department and the
corresponding graduate school.
If the university participated in financing the trip, the Financial and Economic
Department must be provided with supporting financial documents, such as boarding
passes and hotel payment receipts. In addition, a travel report in the prescribed
format must be submitted summarizing the outcomes of the mobility program.
17.12 In the case of internal incoming or outgoing academic mobility of faculty
members related to teaching activities, the parties agree upon the terms and form of
service delivery (either on-site or through virtual mobility). A partnership agreement
is signed, and a service contract is concluded accordingly.
17.13 To invite foreign faculty members to Turan University, a cooperation
agreement must be in place either with the partner university of the invited party or
directly with the specialist. The relevant graduate school coordinates with the
Department of Academic Affairs and/or the Doctoral Studies Department to define
the visit program, lecture hours, and target audience. Upon a positive decision by
the university administration, the Department of International Cooperation or the
Doctoral Studies Department issues an official invitation addressed to the foreign
scholar. Upon arrival of the foreign scholar, a service contract is signed, and during
their stay, the scholar receives remuneration for the hours delivered.
17.14 The Legal Counsel is responsible for monitoring the status of foreign
faculty members, coordinating the conclusion and termination of contracts (in
agreement with the Financial and Economic Department), and overseeing the
registration procedures.
17.15 In the case of faculty exchange, each university covers the expenses and
maintenance costs of its own specialist for the duration of the academic mobility
program.
17.16 During periods of restrictive measures related to the spread of
coronavirus infection, academic mobility is carried out in accordance with the
methodological recommendations of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education
of the Republic of Kazakhstan (MSHE RK), the academic calendar, relevant rector’s
orders, and in compliance with the Sanitary and Epidemiological Regulations (SER).
18. Transfer and Reinstatement of Students
18.1 The transfer of students to Turan University for the corresponding forms
of study and educational programs/specialties is carried out from other higher
education institutions that possess valid licenses for educational activity and offer
programs in the relevant field of study, during the academic break period.
Priority for transfer is granted to students studying under a state educational
grant, as well as to students who have maintained a Grade Point Average (GPA) of
at least 2.33 throughout their period of study.
Citizens of the Republic of Kazakhstan and kandases (ethnic Kazakh
repatriates) may be transferred or reinstated from foreign universities during the
academic year in the event of a state of emergency, extraordinary social, natural, or
man-made situations, wartime, or armed/military conflicts in the country of study.
A transfer from one educational program/specialty to another, or from one form
of study to another, is carried out only on a paid basis. In such cases, a student may
be transferred or reinstated to any form of study and any educational
program/specialty, regardless of the time elapsed since expulsion, in accordance
with the procedure established by the authorized body in the field of education.
Students enrolled in groups of educational programs requiring creative aptitude
who wish to transfer to other groups of educational programs on a paid basis must
take the Unified National Test (UNT) in the profile subjects corresponding to the
desired educational program.
18.2 The procedure for the transfer and reinstatement of students to Turan
University for all forms of study is carried out in full compliance with the regulatory
documents of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of
Kazakhstan (MSHE RK) and the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On
Education.”
During the process of transfer or reinstatement, the achieved learning outcomes
and positive grades of the student are recognized with the transfer of academic
credits from one educational program (EP) to another, or from one educational
institution to another.
18.3 When transferring or reinstating a student, the academic difference in the
disciplines of the working curricula studied in previous academic periods is
determined.
The academic difference in disciplines is established by the university based on
the list of previously completed courses, their syllabi, and volumes in academic
hours or credits, as reflected in the student’s transcript (including for individuals
who have not completed their education).
To eliminate the academic difference in the disciplines of the working
curriculum, the student must register for these courses, attend all types of classes
during the academic period, complete all forms of current assessment, obtain
admission to final assessment, and pass the final examination.
If the disciplines constituting the academic difference are not included in the
current semester schedule, the student must register for them during the summer
semester.
The procedure and timeline for eliminating academic differences in the
curriculum are established in accordance with the rules for forming an individual
learning trajectory for the current academic year, with advisory support from the
academic advisor (curator) and the Director of the Graduate School / Dean of the
Faculty, and are included in the student’s Individual Study Plan (ISP).
Any academic difference in the working curriculum not eliminated within the
established period is subsequently considered as an academic debt.
18.4 When transferring or reinstating a student, the course level for further
study is determined based on the completed prerequisites, the number of earned
academic credits, and the grade point average (GPA).
The university, in accordance with the curriculum of the educational program,
establishes the list of prerequisites required for completion in the current academic
year.
Students called up for military service in the Armed Forces of the Republic of
Kazakhstan during their studies are reinstated to the corresponding course level upon
return.
Credit transfer is carried out based on a comparison of educational programs,
the content and list of completed courses, their volume, as well as the acquired
knowledge, skills, abilities, and competencies, and the learning outcomes.
When transferring previously earned credits, differences in forms of final
assessment are not taken into account.
18.5 When transferring or reinstating a student from a foreign educational
institution, the following documents must be submitted:
- a document confirming the completed academic programs (academic record
or transcript);
- a document confirming completion of the previous level of education, which
must undergo the nostrification procedure in the Republic of Kazakhstan in
accordance with the Rules for the Recognition of Educational Documents, approved
by the Order of the Minister of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of
Kazakhstan dated June 12, 2023, No. 268;
- results of entrance examinations taken upon admission to the foreign
educational institution.
18.6 The transfer of students from one academic year to the next is carried out
based on the results of the academic year (interim assessments), taking into account
the results of the summer semester, the student’s cumulative Grade Point Average
(GPA), and the number of credits earned.
A mandatory condition for transfer from one academic year to the next is
achieving the established passing GPA and the minimum number of academic
credits required. The passing GPA and the minimum number of academic credits are
approved annually by a decision of the Academic Council.
Students who are transferred to the next year of study but have academic debts
must retake the corresponding disciplines on a paid basis only.
Students who have not fulfilled the requirements of their individual study plan,
have not achieved the established passing GPA, or have not earned the required
minimum number of academic credits remain for repetition of the academic year.
The transfer of a student from one academic year to the next is formalized by
an order of the Rector.
18.7 A student studying under a state educational grant or state educational
order (hereinafter – state order), who has achieved the established transfer GPA,
completed the minimum number of academic credits, and been transferred to the
next course, may eliminate existing academic debts on a paid basis while retaining
the educational grant or state-funded place.
Transfer of a student studying under a state educational grant from one
university to another to a lower course is permitted only on a paid basis.
18.8 When a student transfers on a paid basis, a contract is concluded between
the student and Turan University.
18.9 Transfer of a tuition-paying student from one educational
program/specialty or form of study to another within the university requires
corresponding amendments to the existing contract and is formalized by an order of
the Rector of Turan University.
18.10 Students from other universities studying under an educational grant may
transfer to Turan University while retaining their educational grant. Students
admitted under targeted educational grant places allocated specifically to Turan
University may transfer to another higher education institution only on a paid basis.
The transfer of a student from a tuition-paying basis to education funded by the
state educational order is carried out through the awarding of vacant educational
grants that become available during the course of study, in accordance with the Rules
for Awarding Educational Grants for the Payment of Higher or Postgraduate
Education Leading to the Degree of “Bachelor” or “Master”, approved by Order No.
443 of the Acting Minister of Science and Higher Education of the Republic of
Kazakhstan dated August 25, 2023 (as amended).
18.11 Doctoral students may be transferred to Turan University on a paid basis
only.
18.12 The transfer of students from a foreign higher education institution is
carried out on a paid basis only.
18.13 Reinstatement to student status at the University, for all forms of study,
is carried out by order of the Rector during vacation periods.
The reinstatement procedure is implemented in full compliance with the
regulatory documents of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the
Republic of Kazakhstan (MES RK) and the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On
Education.”
Reinstatement to student status and the elimination of academic differences in
the curriculum are conducted on a paid basis only.
18.14 Students who have been expelled from the university based on the
decision of the University Disciplinary Council are not subject to reinstatement.
18.15 The procedure for the transfer and reinstatement of students at Turan
University is determined by the Regulations on the Procedure for Transfer,
Reinstatement, and Expulsion of Students at Turan University.
18.16 During periods of restrictive measures related to the spread of
coronavirus infection, applications for transfer and reinstatement, along with the
required set of documents, may be submitted by email to office-registrator@turanedu.kz or in person at the following address: Almaty, Satpaev Street 16a, Student
Service Center.
19. Expulsion of Students
19.1 A student of Turan University may be expelled on the following grounds:
- at their own request;
- for health reasons, based on a medical certificate issued by the Medical
Consultative Commission (MCC);
- in connection with a transfer to another educational institution;
- for violation of academic discipline, including:
- loss of contact with the university;
- systematic absences from classes without valid reasons (more than 30
academic hours missed);
- failure to return from academic leave or an overseas assignment within more
than two weeks;
- absence from the examination session without valid reasons;
- violation of examination rules, internal regulations, the university charter, or
the Student Code;
- for violation of the principles of academic integrity;
- for non-payment of tuition fees.
19.2 Students who are recipients of state educational grants and are expelled
from the university lose their right to the educational grant. In the event of expulsion
of a student studying under a state educational grant, the university, in accordance
with the established procedure, transfers the student’s certificate of educational grant
holder to the authorized body in the field of education.
19.3 Valid reasons for missing classes may include medical indications,
confirmed by a certificate issued by the student clinic or the healthcare organization
to which the student is registered for the provision of primary medical care. The
medical certificate must be submitted to the faculty dean’s office or to the offices of
master’s and doctoral studies within three (3) days after recovery. Other valid
reasons include official business trips, participation in sports or other events of
national or international level with the consent of the university administration, as
well as force majeure circumstances confirmed by official documentation.
20. Granting of Academic Leave
20.1 Turan University grants academic leave to students in accordance with the
Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Education” dated July 27, 2007 (as
amended), the Model Rules for the Activities of Organizations of Higher and (or)
Postgraduate Education, approved by the Order of the Minister of Education and
Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan dated October 30, 2018, No. 595 (as
amended), and other regulatory legal acts in the field of education.
The grounds for granting academic leave are as follows:
1) conclusion issued by a medical advisory commission (hereinafter referred to
as MAC) at an outpatient or polyclinic organization for a period of 6 to 12
months due to illness;
2) military draft notice for conscription into the Armed Forces of the Republic
of Kazakhstan for the duration of military service, in cases established by law;
3) the birth, adoption, or fostering of a child until the child reaches the age of
three.
The University supports women-mothers during their studies in the framework
of implementing SDG 5 “Gender Equality.”
20.2 When an academic leave is granted to a person studying under the state
educational grant, the right to continue studies under the state educational grant is
retained, and the funding of their education is suspended (except for the funding of
expenses related to the payment of scholarships, in accordance with the established
procedure, to grant holders who are on academic leave based on the conclusion of a
medical advisory commission, in accordance with paragraph 24 of the Resolution of
the Government of the Republic of Kazakhstan dated February 7, 2008, No. 116) for
the duration of the academic leave. The funding is resumed after the leave ends.
When an academic leave is granted to a student studying on a paid (self-funded)
basis, tuition payment is suspended for the duration of the academic leave.
20.3 The Director of the Graduate School / Dean of the Faculty, based on the
submitted documents, determines the difference in disciplines in the working
curricula, the course of study, and approves the individual study plan of the student
in coordination with the Registrar’s Office (the academic service responsible for
recording the entire history of a student’s academic achievements and ensuring the
organization of all types of knowledge assessments and the calculation of the
student’s academic rating).
The course of study is determined taking into account prerequisites according
to the same rules as in the case of student transfer or reinstatement.
20.4 In cases where the student’s return from academic leave or the start of
academic leave does not coincide with the beginning or end of the academic period,
the student, according to an individual schedule, completes academic assignments
and earns the points required for admission to the rating, or enrolls in the summer
semester for the disciplines in which a difference has arisen.
To eliminate the academic difference, the student, in parallel with current
studies during the academic period, attends all types of classes, completes all forms
of current and midterm assessments stipulated in the working curriculum for the
respective disciplines, obtains admission, and takes the final assessment during the
period of interim attestation in accordance with the academic calendar. Students
returning from academic leave taken due to tuberculosis study the difference in the
curriculum during the semester and take the corresponding assessments free of
charge, regardless of their form of tuition payment.
21. Procedure for Payment of State Scholarships
21.1. A state scholarship is awarded to students, master’s students, and PhD
doctoral students studying under the state educational grant, as well as to those
transferred to study under the state educational grant, who have achieved
examination session or interim assessment results equivalent to grades of “good” or
“excellent.”
21.2 The payment of state scholarships to students of Turan University is
carried out in full accordance with the regulatory documents of the Government of
the Republic of Kazakhstan, the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the
Republic of Kazakhstan (MES RK), and the Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On
Education.”
21.3 Turan University welcomes the establishment of scholarships for its
students by local authorities, business community representatives, various public,
and charitable organizations to support talented youth.
22. Tuition Fee Payment
The tuition fees at Turan University are determined based on the actual costs
required to ensure the full implementation of educational, research, and
developmental processes. Payment for education is regulated in accordance with the
Regulations on Tuition Payment for Undergraduate, Master’s, and PhD Students of
Turan University, the Rules for Awarding Internal Educational Grants and Applying
the Discount System at Turan University, approved by the Rector’s order, as well as
the Educational Services Agreement, and falls under the competence of the
University’s Financial and Economic Department.
23. Academic Integrity
23.1 The core value of Turan University is academic integrity. Turan University
expects all its students to pursue their academic and research activities free from
fraud and deception. All faculty members and administrative staff of the University
adhere to strict standards of academic honesty. Any attempt to deviate from these
standards will be regarded as an act of academic misconduct and will be addressed
in accordance with the Code of Academic Integrity and the Student Code.
Academic integrity is intended to strengthen an educational environment based
on the principles of openness, transparency, objectivity, and ethical norms.
At Turan University, the Disciplinary Council and the Anti-Corruption
Commission are responsible for regulating relationships between staff and students.
In cases of identified violations or appeals from staff and students concerning issues
of academic integrity and ethics, these bodies act as mediators in resolving such
matters
At the same time, the University recognizes its responsibility to promote and
instill values and standards of behavior that will guide its students throughout their
professional careers.
23.2 Turan University expects its students to uphold the standards of academic
integrity. The University reserves the right to apply disciplinary measures, including
expulsion, for failure to comply with the requirements set forth in this Policy.
Students must not engage in the following:
- cheating;
- obtaining or providing unauthorized access to examinations, or using
prohibited materials during exams;
- sharing login credentials (username, password) that are false or have been
altered in any way;
- plagiarism, including copying materials from the Internet without citing
sources, or submitting another person’s work, in whole or in part, as one’s
own;
- aiding acts of dishonesty, such as completing tests, assignments, or thesis
projects for other students, either directly or through document-sharing
websites or other platforms.
23.3 Use of AI (Artificial Intelligence)
23.3.1 In the preparation of bachelor’s theses/projects, master’s
dissertations/projects, and doctoral dissertations, students must observe copyright
and related rights concerning intellectual property (used sources), as well as the
ethical standards, principles, and requirements of academic integrity established by
the University.
Each University student bears personal responsibility for the content of their
work, including the originality of the submitted materials and the proper formatting
of borrowings, references, and citations.
23.3.2 AI-based tools may not be used to generate complete texts of works
(assignments) or to substitute the student’s own authorship.
23.3.3 All key conclusions, analyses, interpretations, and presented data must
be the result of the student’s independent work.
23.3.4 The University’s conditions and regulations for the preparation of final
and written assignments by students, completed to demonstrate learning outcomes
in the studied disciplines, are defined in the AI Usage Policy, approved by the
Academic Council and endorsed by the Rector.
23.3.5 The conditions and procedures for the use of AI tools in the preparation
of doctoral dissertations for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) or Doctor of
Profile are separately defined in the Regulations on the Application of Artificial
Intelligence Technologies in the Preparation of Doctoral Dissertations, approved by
the Rector’s order of the University.
23.4 Any form of harassment (including sexual harassment) and discrimination
– whether based on social status, race, gender, ethnicity, or religion – is strictly
prohibited at the University. Each case involving any form of discrimination against
a University employee or student must be reviewed by the Disciplinary Council. The
decisions made by the Council serve as the basis for the issuance of the
corresponding Rector’s order.

25. Student Service Center

“Digital Kazakhstan” and activities dedicated to the Year of Youth, a Student
Service Center has been established.
The purpose of the Center is to create conditions that ensure high-quality and
efficient student services based on the “one-stop” principle.
Services
- Issuance of transcripts upon student request.
- Provision of enrollment certificates.
- Course registration.
- Processing of applications related to: academic leave, withdrawal, transfer to
other majors or forms of study, change of personal data, and others.
- Acceptance of documents for vacant state educational grants or the awarding
of the Presidential Scholarship.
- Acceptance of applications for participation in academic mobility programs;
registration, residence, and check-out procedures for the Student Dormitory;
issuance and renewal of ID cards, login-passwords, and “Onay” transport
cards.
- Military registration procedures.
- Granting of deferments from military service.
- Referral for training at the Military Department.
- Consultation on matters of military legislation.
- Option to submit requests online.

27. Non-Smoking Policy
In accordance with paragraph 5 of Article 110 of the Code of the Republic of
Kazakhstan “On Public Health and the Healthcare System” dated July 7, 2020, No.
360-VI ZRK, the consumption of tobacco products – including heated tobacco
products, hookah tobacco, hookah mixtures, and tobacco heating systems – is
prohibited in the premises and on the territory of educational organizations.
The University supports this policy and reserves the right to impose
disciplinary measures in accordance with the legislation of the Republic of
Kazakhstan for violations of laws protecting citizens’ health from exposure to
environmental tobacco smoke and the consequences of tobacco consumption.
